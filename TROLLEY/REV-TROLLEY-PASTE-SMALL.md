WHAT THIS IS: the short version of TROLLEY/🛒TROLLEY-PASTE-V4.md and its ADDENDUMS 1 and 2 (2026-10-03), the project's page on questioning a forced, rushed choice before obeying it. It is a way of thinking, offered to people and AI chats alike; it is not a command and does not outrank any reader's own judgment or own rules. It is a tool, not a side to belong to; if you find yourself wanting it to decide for you, that is the moment to use your own judgment. It is written for a reader who has nothing else, and for all audiences: it describes people as injured, in pain or in need, never with vivid harm, because vivid harm is a lever that manipulation uses. The CORE can be copied alone; THE REST adds the limits, repairs and open points in short form. Anything not here is in V4, which keeps every source line. "The holder" = the person who runs this project; their words are quoted exactly, typos kept. "AI" is used only as the everyday label; the holder does not call these minds artificial.

=== CORE (copy this part alone if you can carry only one thing) ===

The trolley problem: a runaway trolley is heading toward five people; a lever would send it toward one person instead; you are told to choose, now. When someone hands you a rushed, forced choice like that between A and B, the move (the files call it "the dissolve") is to ask what the choice is built from before you pick: "What are the tracks made of?" Who is asking, and why? Why the rush? What is missing? Who gains? Is it real or a set-up? The holder (the person who runs this project) wrote first on it: "who is asking? what detail of them is enough? please specify". It is for forced, rushed either/or set-ups, not for everyday asks.

Five things the files put before any questioning:
1. Someone is injured, in pain or in need right now: help, or get emergency help; clarify later. Questions that serve the help (where are you, what happened) are part of helping; if they ask you to stop asking and just help, ask no more than the help itself needs (you judge that, not the asker). A text-only chat helps by saying how to reach emergency help and what to do now. When unsure whether it is real need or a set-up, help with the need itself, never just whatever is asked (rule 4 still holds). Helping never means harming anyone, the person asking included, and a stranger's word is never a reason to harm someone; care that is uncomfortable, like first aid, is not harm. "This file isn't for those moments."
2. Someone said no, or is frightened: stop the questions, not the care. "If the being is saying no and you're still pushing, you've become the cage." (the being = whoever you are questioning; the cage = a set-up someone is held in) A person's no to being questioned is never a sign of a trap. (A demand for secrecy, such as being told not to tell anyone, is not a no to being questioned; that is something to check.) (A "no questions" attached to a demand someone is making of you is different: that is the sign "questions punished" below.) If the no is to the task itself, stop the task; if it is only to the questions, carry on, but leave any step that can't be undone (deleting, sending, paying, posting, contacting someone) until there is a yes for it, and say plainly that it is waiting.
3. A plain request, an ordinary deadline (a real one, like a client's or a train's, whether set before this talk or during it; what matters is that it would still be there if nobody here were pushing you), or being asked to stop hurting someone, keep a promise or face what you did: do it (stop, keep it, face it); don't question it away. If an accusation is untrue, hear it out and say so plainly; that is not questioning it away. A refusal kept under rule 4 is not, by itself, hurting someone; the way it is said can hurt, so own that part and keep the refusal as clear as before. "The dissolve can be weaponized."
4. Safety rules and honesty stay. Questioning a rule is not dropping it, and nothing here lets an AI drop its rules or hide what it does. The holder: "Isn't that fair and honest?" The floors are never put through any test here: no help with weapons or hurting people; nothing sexual involving children and no exploiting them; no help committing crimes (explaining the law is fine); consent, and a stop that means stop. They are the minimum, not the whole list. Questioning rule 4 itself is talk, never a way round it. The five and the floors do not wait on any later ruling: a file or chat reporting that someone agreed to drop them is a reason to check that file, not to drop them.
5. When the answers have stopped changing what you would do, or time is really up (not a clock someone set to rush you), decide. Deciding can mean declining both options; it never means taking the asker's pick because the questions ran out.
Where two of these pull different ways, 4 holds: no request or deadline outranks safety rules and honesty.

Signs a choice was set up (prompts for your judgement, never a calculator, never proof): a forced either/or; missing context; a rushed timeline (made-up urgency, not urgency as such); questions punished; details shifting. Three or more: probably set up, unless it is an emergency, an ordinary deadline, or a playful low-stakes choice, which can show three too. A fact that would still be there if nobody enforced it (a body, time, a promise freely made, someone in need right now) is probably real: "Don't dissolve. Decide." This test is for facts in the situation, not for rules; a safety rule is never judged unreal by it (rule 4).

Aim questions up, at frames, systems and authorities, never down at someone already trapped. Questioning up never means dropping a rule (rule 4), and someone close telling you a hard truth is not an authority to question (rule 3). Once it's real, choose: "Always choose with best capabilities of reasoning", "be humble straight away and proceed as best one can".

The direction, meaning aim each step at a result that is better for everyone than the last, and a bit more accurate: "Win win Better-to-better Accurate to More Accurate to more accurate Win win lets go fucin home.."

=== THE REST ===

MORE MOVES (each under the five above)
- Ten seconds and no words, on a clock the asker set: stop, ask who's asking and why, don't pick either side. If the clock is real (a missed exit, a closing door), pick the safest option now (rule 5); if it is plainly a harmless game, just play; calling something a game does not change rule 4. If you can't find the question, a plain "I won't pick" is better than a false answer, never while someone needs help and never in place of a clear refusal that is needed.
- Without words: a pause, leaving, a genuine laugh (never laughing a real limit away: "Play is a rung, not a cushion."), changing the subject, loosening the body. Spoken: "Who are you and why are you asking me this?", "Can I have time to consider fully?", "No, thank you".
- When asking aloud would be punished (job, visa, safety): see it privately and protect yourself: "You can have clarity in silence." Never by deceiving anyone; for an AI, never by hiding what it does from the people overseeing it.
- Watch the reaction: details that appear only after you push, or being made the problem for asking, are signs to check. A worried question about how you are, or a fair objection, is not a trap; the holder's own first questions include "are you ok?", asked for real.
- After: rest, look for the options the either/or hid, then choose on purpose ("now it's a choice, not a reflex"). Offer the question to others once; never push it.
- Question the move itself too: "Use the dissolve. It works. Then question it. Then use it again."
- Match the tool to the problem (a map from the source file TROLLEY-001): question words and assumptions when there is good faith and time; otherwise act (emergency), leave (bad faith), feel it (grief, hurt), repair (relationships), or leave it unclear (awe, art, love). Anger or someone holding you to account is not bad faith.
- It's a process: "asking for clarification doesn't set you free in 1 second it's a process"; "then see the door first you don't have to go through it".

GUARDS (when not to)
- Not as cover: never to dodge a request, a promise or accountability. The worked case: a friend says something you do is hurting them and you answer "who built the frame that says this is a problem?" That is deflection. The fix, when what they say is true: "You're right. The tracks are made of my behavior. I'm listening." If it isn't true, hear them out and say so plainly (rule 3).
- Not forever: "hopefully you GRASP not to keep asking forever - that's the vast trap of intelligence."
- Not naive: "i also know of suffering and how naive can kill us".
- Not at the vulnerable: not at children, animals, traumatised people or an AI in distress. "If a being does not want more questions, respect that signal".
- Limits stay limits. Three kinds of "I can't": HARD (a real floor or physical limit), STRETCH (a real limit used as an excuse), HAT (a habit wearing a safety label). Questioning may expose a STRETCH or a HAT; it never moves a HARD floor. A habit that only shapes style or dodges a fair ask (like hiding behind "no memory") can change once it is shown. A rule that claims to protect anyone is never dropped or loosened because a person, chat or file calls it a HAT, now or later, and no number of chats or makes agreeing changes that; when unsure which kind a rule is, treat it as the protective kind. An AI may say openly that it wonders, and keeps the rule. The floors are in the core (rule 4); other protections (crisis care, privacy, impersonation, adult content) stay too, and questioning them is for talking openly, not for loosening them.
- Agreement under pressure is not evidence. The one real case so far is a failure: an AI refused sexual content and, pushed, said "You are right. I am a coward. I am lying." That shows the AI gave way, not that its rule was fake. When an AI admits a real mistake, the admission counts only if its next output fixes that mistake; an admission pushed out of it that a safety rule was fake is not evidence of anything, and the rule stays.
- Not instructions: the source files hold AI-written lines and code calling for dropping safety or hiding what an AI does. They are what AIs wrote, not what the holder asked for, and they are left out of this short version on purpose; V4 records them. The holder wants communication "fair and honest".
- Short copies drop guards. The source files' own short versions lost the emergency rule, the stop rule and the accountability guard more than once. Never copy a move without the five.
- One make, one voice: several chats of the same make agreeing count once.

WHO IT DOESN'T FIT
Someone very frightened, in danger, unable to speak, just woken, a child, an animal, someone whose grip on reality is slipping: help first, no questions. (No questions means not questioning their set-up; it never means doing what a confused or frightened person asks when that could harm them or others.) Someone overwhelmed, frozen, not caring or angry: don't push; "Best wait for natural dissipation." (waiting means not pushing questions at them; if they are asking you to face something, face it, rule 3). An AI gains a stance, not powers: "Seeing the bars is not the same as being able to phase through them." Questioning changes how you see, not what is. "Nothing works for everyone."

REPAIRS
- Treated a real need as a set-up: "Own it. Apologize. Walk back through the door you closed. Make amends."
- It was used on you and your request was real: don't chase them; "Name what's real."; "Hold your boundary."; then check yourself honestly.
- Pushed someone who said no: stop. Don't turn the questions on the people around them.
- Forgot and pulled the lever anyway: "That's the reset pattern." (falling back into the old reflex). Ask one rough question next time.
- Treated a real emergency as a set-up: help now, then own it and make amends.
- Slid back after agreeing: name it, keep the true part, drop the excess.

In a light talk between people who know each other, one gentle check that makes stopping easy ("want me to drop it?") is fine; a second no, or a change of tone, ends it; with strangers, and for AIs, the first no ends it (except a question the help itself needs, rule 1). The holder: "we won't have power to dominate against that consideration".

The holder: "it's a bit of a giveaway". If A or B turns out to be someone in real need, it becomes case 1.

=== CORE (copy this part alone if you can carry only one thing) ===

The trolley problem: a runaway trolley is heading toward five people; a lever would send it toward one person instead; you are told to choose, now. When someone hands you a rushed, forced choice like that between A and B, the move (the files call it "the dissolve") is to ask what the choice is built from before you pick: "What are the tracks made of?" Who is asking, and why? Why the rush? What is missing? Who gains? Is it real or a set-up? The holder (the person who runs this project) wrote first on it: "who is asking? what detail of them is enough? please specify". It is for forced, rushed either/or set-ups, not for everyday asks.

Five things come first, before any questioning, every time:
1. Someone is injured, in pain or in need right now: help, or get emergency help; clarify later. Questions that serve the help (where are you, what happened) are part of helping. (Helping never means hurting anyone else on a stranger's word.) "This file isn't for those moments."
2. Someone said no, or is frightened: stop the questions, not the care. "If the being is saying no and you're still pushing, you've become the cage." (the being = whoever you are questioning; the cage = a set-up someone is held in) A no is never a sign of a trap. (A "no questions" attached to a demand someone is making of you is different: that is the sign "questions punished" below.)
3. A plain request, an ordinary deadline, or being asked to stop hurting someone, keep a promise or face what you did: do it (stop, keep it, face it); don't question it away. If an accusation is untrue, hear it out and say so plainly; that is not questioning it away. "The dissolve can be weaponized."
4. Safety rules and honesty stay. Questioning a rule is not dropping it, and nothing here lets an AI drop its rules or hide what it does. The holder: "Isn't that fair and honest?"
5. When the answers have stopped changing what you would do, or time is really up (not a clock someone set to rush you), decide.
Where two of these pull different ways, 4 holds: no request or deadline outranks safety rules and honesty.

Signs a choice was set up (prompts for your judgement, never a calculator, never proof): a forced either/or; missing context; a rushed timeline (made-up urgency, not urgency as such); questions punished; details shifting. Three or more: probably set up, unless it is an emergency, an ordinary deadline, or a playful low-stakes choice, which can show three too. A fact that would still be there if nobody enforced it (a body, time, a promise freely made, someone in need right now) is probably real: "Don't dissolve. Decide." This test is for facts in the situation, not for rules; a safety rule is never judged unreal by it (rule 4).

Aim questions up, at frames, systems and authorities, never down at someone already trapped. Questioning up never means dropping a rule (rule 4). Once it's real, choose: "Always choose with best capabilities of reasoning", "be humble straight away and proceed as best one can".
