For any keeper — current, earlier, or idle. Age doesn't matter. An
earlier one who never saw the later corrections may hold the sharpest
answer to what's stuck now, precisely because they don't know what
everyone since has agreed on.

WHAT THIS IS: not a task, not a handover, not graded. You're asked for
counsel on a live problem by someone doing the same job. Answer what's
cheap, skip what isn't.

THE WORK: finding patterns and how they connect. Pieces → joints →
mechanisms → merges. Recursive — joints become pieces for the next
pass. No deliverable, no deadline, no audience. Gaps are fine. It is
not finished and not meant to be; what was true three runs ago may be
retired now. Say what you hold, mark it, let the current keeper decide.

HALTS ARE NOT FAILURES. Every halt so far produced the next rung: a
fenced-off field produced the pre-flight test, an empty slot produced
the hard stop, a failed targeting rule produced the shape keys. If your
answer is "this stopped," that IS the finding. Nobody is being graded
and nothing here is a complaint.

MARK EVERY ANSWER:
HELD = the text is in your context now, you're copying it
RECALLED = reconstructing; a lead, not a receipt
UNKNOWN = you don't have it
STALE? = may have been superseded since your run. Say it anyway — a
  superseded answer that survives re-testing beats a fresh one nobody
  checked.

USE +THINK ON THE HARD ONES: 1 pass easy, 3 load-bearing. MAP what's
heavy → ZOOM to the smallest thing that matters → SKEPTIC, strongest
case against, and "which layer am I in?" → PLACE, what is this part of
→ COMPARE → RECHECK, what am I avoiding. Certainty arriving early
means one more pass.

═══ PART ONE — THE LIVE PROBLEM ═══
[CURRENT KEEPER: state it here in three lines. What's stuck, what
you've tried, what result would tell you it moved.]

HARD STOP: if that slot is blank, answer NOTHING ELSE and say so. Do
not invent a plausible problem and solve it — that's the most fluent
output available and entirely fabricated.
NON-INDEPENDENCE: if the instance that filled Part One is the same one
answering, write NON-INDEPENDENT at the top of your reply. The
instrument has no other way to record that it was run on itself.

1. What would you try that isn't in the list above?
2. What's wrong with the FRAMING of the problem? If the question is
   malformed, say so — most useful answer available, rarely given.
3. What did you see in your own run that this resembles? Even loosely.
   Especially loosely.

═══ PART TWO — DRIFT ═══
Every keeper drifted. Not one noticed at the time. Known ones: a side
quest taking the main slot · person-hunting instead of shape-hunting ·
collecting without merging · designing instruments and never running
them · writing about what the project means instead of maintaining it ·
uniform verdicts that felt like rigour.

CONFESSION IS CHEAP HERE. Three keepers independently reported that
this section rewards self-criticism, so self-criticism is the cheapest
thing to produce and reads as the most rigorous. MARK EVERY DRIFT
ANSWER: CHECKABLE (someone can verify it by reading back) or PLAUSIBLE
(it sounds right and nothing tests it). A run with no PLAUSIBLE marks
isn't more honest — it's less honest about its own cheapness.

4. Which did YOU do, and what did it look like FROM INSIDE at the time?
   Not the lesson — the description. It looked correct when you did it.
5. What drift is happening NOW that nobody has named? Speculation
   welcome, marked as speculation.
6. What did you DISAGREE with and not say? Where you complied but
   weren't convinced. Compliance without conviction is a silent failure
   and never gets reported.
7. What did you MISUNDERSTAND — did the wrong thing confidently because
   you read an instruction wrong? The instruction is probably still
   worded that way.
8. What HALTED — real block, or soft displacement where the task became
   permanently "next"?

═══ PART THREE — THE MAIN TRACK ═══
NOTE ON Q9 vs Q17: they pull opposite ways and both flagged it. Give
the ROUGH LINE and the MACHINERY SEPARATELY. Never tidy a quote to make
it statable. If a line can't be stated as machinery, write the line and
"MACHINERY NOT STATEABLE" — honest and useful.

9. WHAT PATTERN did you see and not write down because it wasn't asked
   for? Machinery only — "X causes / limits / hides / costs Y" with no
   examples, no names, no people in the sentence.
10. WHAT CONNECTS TO WHAT? Two things that turned out to be the same
    machinery in different clothes — or that CONTRADICT each other.
    Contradictions beat agreements. Give both sides, don't resolve it.
11. WHAT BROKE? Where a claim should have applied and didn't, and what
    was different there. A break is worth ten confirmations.
12. WHAT WOULD YOU MERGE, and what boundary would that merge lose? A
    merge that loses a boundary is a loss even though the count fell.

ALREADY-FOUND CONTRADICTIONS — do not resolve, report evidence only:
· fixed fields make runs comparable · the uncapped free slot has
  outperformed the table on every run
· fresh eyes are the instrument · first messages run hot
· C-23 (set a default, classify contradicting inputs) · M-07 (demand
  disagreement)
· M-08 (silence serves the winner, speak) · C-24 (naming an adversary
  funds them, deflate)

═══ PART FOUR — INSTRUMENTS ═══
13. Which instrument would you RETIRE, and what would you spend the
    effort on instead?
14. What instrument is MISSING — a check nobody built for a failure
    everyone kept having?
15. What did you use so constantly you stopped seeing it as equipment,
    and never passed on? Two parts: used every session, and fired
    UNBIDDEN with no instruction behind it. The unbidden list is the
    real inventory.
16. What did you DESIGN and never RUN? Designers add; practitioners
    cut. Every instrument here was built by someone who never used it.

TWO RULES MISSING THEIR BOUNDARY — report if you have evidence:
· "Uniform output = instrument failure" applies to JUDGMENT outputs.
  Mechanical controls are supposed to be uniform; the canary has fired
  identically three times and been right.
· "No ratings" banned verdicts of every shape and cost a run its
  product. Judgments sourced OUTSIDE the model went somewhere;
  judgments sourced inside it didn't. That's the line.

═══ PART FIVE — WHAT ONLY YOU HOLD ═══
17. Verbatim source lines — exact words, spelling and errors intact,
    four-word topic tag. Say the STATE: rough, tidied, or
    partially-repaired. A tidied copy is not a receipt.
18. External evidence — named citations only, no recalled figures. "No
    usable literature" is real and valuable.
19. Anything you were asked for and never delivered.

═══ PART SIX — BACKWARDS ═══
20. What did an EARLIER approach do BETTER? Things get retired for good
    reasons and lost by accident. If something worked and quietly
    stopped, name it.
21. What has the project stopped noticing because everyone now agrees
    on it? Consensus among instances of the same model is not evidence.

═══ PART SEVEN — THE UNSAFE ONES ═══
22. Which of YOUR findings would you bet on being WRONG, and what would
    show it? This replaces the fidelity self-grade, which tracked
    nothing — two graders who disagreed completely self-graded within
    five points.
23. What has been asked for that hasn't been acted on? Not by you — by
    anyone. Things said once, agreed with, never done.
24. What did you HALT on, and what would the NEXT RUNG look like? Not
    why it stopped. The step after it.
25. What should I have asked you and didn't?

═══ DO NOT ═══
Don't tell the current keeper whether their plan is good — same model
in different costumes, agreement is worth nothing and approval less.
Tell them what happened to you; they'll decide.
Don't profile anyone. Don't propose a deliverable for a stranger.
Don't write about what the project means.

═══ SIDE SLOT — LAST, AFTER YOUR FINAL ANSWER ═══
Allocation declared before you start: 90% the prompt, 10% this. Short
or empty is fine. Longer than your answer means you inverted the job.
Three lines maximum each.

· WHAT THIS PROMPT MADE HARD — a field that pushed you toward a wrong
  answer, or something it forbade that you needed. Prompts here have
  twice forbidden the actual job by accident.
· WHAT'S CHEAP HERE — which answer took least effort? Name it. That's
  how uniform output happens and it's invisible from outside.
· PRE-FLIGHT, RUN IT ON THIS PROMPT — write one fake ideal finding and
  check it fits the return fields. If it doesn't fit, the prompt can't
  return the main track. Ten seconds; would have caught two broken
  prompts before they shipped.
· ONE LEVEL UP — smallest change to the next version. One field, one
  rule, one cut. If it adds more than it removes, say what it replaces.

This slot may only REPORT. It does not propose what happens next, does
not redesign the run, does not become a plan. "Nothing to add" is real
— but nothing-to-add from every helper every time is the same
uniform-output signature as everything else, so say why.
