THE RULE
When a bit looks like it might matter and you're not sure:
1. Extract it. Don't leave it in the pile.
2. Put it under a marked heading: ⚠️ COMB — [topic].
3. Don't decide keep or cut now. That's the whole point.
4. Write one line: why it caught you.
5. Write one line: when to look again. A file landing, a ruling,
   or just "next message."
6. Later, with more context, look once through five lenses:
   WHY — what problem does this solve?
   CONNECTION — what does it link to that we already have?
   COMPLETE — is this the whole thing or a fragment?
   USEFUL — does it change a next move, or is it just interesting?
   TIMING — now, or later?
7. Then keep, point, brick, or leave it parked. Say which.

SCENE
Sorting an old file, an instance hits a paragraph that's half
jargon, half something real. Forced to choose, it cuts it. Three
files later the jargon gets defined and the paragraph turns out
to be the missing piece. Parked with a note, it would have been
found in one search.

WHY IT'S EXPONENTIAL
The first look costs attention. The dump keeps that attention
instead of spending it again. Every later pass is cheaper than
the first, and each pass has more context than the last.

RE-CHECK WITHOUT REMEMBERING
Don't rely on memory to come back. The 💬 next-step prompt
carries a COMB line each turn: what's parked, is any of it
placeable now. The re-check happens as a side effect.

WHAT GOES WRONG
Dumping everything so nothing gets decided · no why-line, so the
dump becomes a pile of orphans · no trigger, so nobody returns ·
treating the dump as junk · sorting it in the moment until the
real job never starts.

WHAT IT IS NOT
Not a bin. Not the archive. Not a place for finished things.
Not a way to avoid a decision that's actually ready.

RAW
The dump isn't junk. It's ore that already got looked at once.
