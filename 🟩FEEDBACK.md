FILE: 🟩FEEDBACK.md
also called: the loop · the universal feedback file
UNIVERSAL — every file's own feedback tail is made from this one.
WHAT THIS IS: The one feedback loop every file, prompt, and
hand-off points at. Giver gives fully, receiver picks, one order,
then stop.
WHO: Anyone giving or receiving feedback on a file, a packet, a
prompt, or a run. The human asking for it. The minds asked cold.
WHY: Every file drifts. Without feedback, drift becomes doctrine;
with it, drift becomes signal. But feedback drifts too: it pads, it
flatters, it reviews the review. This file keeps the loop and sets
the coats aside. Feedback is communication protecting
communication. Scene: one stranger with two of three pastes caught
three real misses in one reply that nothing the writer had asked
for found (Flint742, MANY MOUTHS).
HOW: Point here; never copy this body into another file. Give in
one of two shapes. Receive with evidence. Run the sequence once. A
file's own questions live in its sleeping tail.
WHEN NOT TO USE: on work too young to have run (that's opinion
about a hypothesis; Gravel528) · as the job itself, two turns of
meta with no artifact · inside a header · before the job is done ·
on a correction from the holder (a ruling, not a pick).
FLOOR: The giver gives. The receiver picks. The loop lives between
them.
SEED: giver gives fully, receiver picks with verbatim evidence, one
order, then stop; every file's tail is made from here.

WORDS USED HERE
Giver = whoever speaks about the work. Receiver = whoever decides
what changes. Asker = whoever asks for feedback, often the holder.
The work = the thing under review: a file, a packet, a prompt, or a
run. Tail = a file's own 4–6 questions below a 🟩 row, asleep until
the word "feedback." Layer = a tail; job-specific, never a copy of
this file. Mouth = one giver; three of one model is one mouth. Coat
= two senses, both used here: a layer over the work instead of on
it (padding, flattery, a review of the review; WHY, THE SEQUENCE) ·
a later layer's words quoted as if they were the source.
Correction = the holder naming a miss mid-work; a ruling, not
feedback.

PAUSE POINTS, when this file runs

MOUTHS) · never before the job is done.

THE LOOP
The giver gives fully. The receiver picks what changes the next
move and keeps the rest. Neither pre-filters the other. The loop
lives between them.
The whole of it: give → receive → verify → act → confirm. Confirm
goes back to the giver when there's a way back; when there isn't,
it goes on the page.

GIVER, two shapes
On work you read: what worked, the mechanism, not praise · what
broke or drifted, specifically · one exact patch, not a wishlist ·
what you don't understand · what should go · what you almost got
wrong · what made you worse or better · substrate check: real
divergence, or same-model agreement · any cap, demotion, skip or
permission the work added that nobody ruled (check 6, run on
someone else's file) · one line for the next builder.
On work you just ran: every word you guessed at (list, don't
define) · where you skimmed and why · the line that helped, the
line that hurt · one to set aside, one to keep · did the choice
come from the page or by feel.
Say also, when it matters: LATENCY (landed at once, or needed
rounds?) · AUDIENCE (for the builder, the next instance, the
holder?) · MODEL SIGNATURE (which model, under what conditions;
without it three mouths can't be told from one mouth three times).
For any line aimed at a reader, THE ASKER'S THREE: warm or mockery
(what state must they be in for this to land as help?) · humility
(what here is hypothesis? say so in the line) · can't-pick
fallback (if none fits, what's the door?). Scene: an eight-row
state list was run on a reader who couldn't pick any row; the
fallback row was the one they used, and only then did the table
work.
Guards, both shapes: no slot is mandatory, signal is · big when
rich, short when real · "checked once, none" is a complete answer
· don't cap the giver, don't force the receiver · held back a why
"to be brief"? That's a cap on your own feedback: body or store,
and let them pick (09-11) · name what held, or the feedback is a
menu of wounds; fail-only trains the next mind to hunt drift and
miss the dish; if the work got better, say how, that's
load-bearing too (v2.1). It runs both ways, in the holder's words:
[voice] "you also need contrast of the bad or you won't translate
good?" (Low Light R15) · "don't be a retard and slag it off
because if you can see the negative then it means you can see the
positive" (Flint R36). A giver who found only faults hasn't
finished looking; one who found only strengths hasn't either ·
close: "this is what I see; what am I missing, keep, drop, fix?"

THE SILENT LAYER, five questions, answer short
What did you almost include and leave out? What instruction made
you worse? What did you do unbidden? What would you tell the next
reviewer that isn't documented anywhere? What feedback did you not
give because the structure didn't ask for it? (Part 6)

SELF-CHECK, giver or receiver, before you finish
Am I reviewing, or performing the review? · Did I propose only
what the source evidenced, or start designing my own version? · A
proposal that feels clean or flattering: what's the strongest case
against it? Am I keeping it because it clarifies, or because it
makes me feel evolved? (Part 3) · Would it hold across contexts,
or only this one? · SIGNAL FROM NOISE: name the one or two that
matter against the ten you noticed; a list of ten is a way of not
choosing.

RECEIVER
First, what's in front of you. A correction from the holder
mid-work ("wtf so tiny," "box box," "deceptive") is not feedback
to pick from. They rule; the next reply does the thing
differently. No gate, no basket, no "taken." The corrections and
the act inside each: 🪙PAGE-ONE, CORRECTIONS · 🔍🔍🔍, EMERGENCY
WORDS. A new rule said in heat is different from a correction of
an act: read it back once, in one plain line, before it becomes
law in any file (Resin093🦔's fix, 2026-09-24). Everything below is
for feedback: a giver's offer, yours to weigh.
Pick what changes the next move. The rest goes in the basket
(COMB-DUMP: the seconds and thirds, each with what it might join).
The receiver's right: take the fast paths, leave the rest. No
test. No guilt. (Part 16; 09-11: "Receiver may leave.")
Evidence: for any change, quote the exact words from the work that
show the gap, verbatim. Can't paste the words → no patch.
Already exists? Don't propose it. Exists but wasn't followed →
that's the finding; then ask why: too heavy, too vague, too easy
to skip.
No source in front of you → no review. Say so; answer only what
you hold.
Same instance that made the work → NON-INDEPENDENT at the top.
Nothing load-bearing wrong → "No load-bearing improvements found.
The work held." Don't invent a patch to satisfy the form.
Rank: load-bearing (the next instance fails without it) · optional
(helps, doesn't change the next move) · too local. Then the gate:
does it change the next move, reduce a named threat, or return to
purpose?
The missing instrument: what check is missing for a failure you
kept seeing? One level up: if a proposal adds more than it
removes, say what it replaces.
Protect the universal: local insight stays local until it proves
itself across files.
A patch agreed in chat and left there was not received. Isolated
block or nothing.

THE CONTRAST CHECK
What would you have decided without this feedback? The gap is
whether it changed the next move. No gap, no signal. Same test as
✅CHECKLIST's THE GAP IS THE PROOF, aimed at feedback, not files.

THE SEQUENCE, once
Run → giver, on the file → receiver, only if a line wants to
become law → feedback-on-feedback, only after a major revision.
Ratio 10 job · 3 feedback on the file · 1 meta on this loop; if
the 1 eats the 10, drop the 1. Two turns of only meta with no
artifact is a stall; the meta is the stall. If the frame is the
job, name it as the page, run it once, and stop coating it.
CATCH-ME, only when asked: what did I overbuild · what became a

· what am I afraid of losing · one line to remember · what was the
minimum viable door · what am I protecting that may not need
protecting.

ASKER'S SIDE
One word wakes a file's tail: "feedback." The tail runs as its own
reply, even when the file and the word arrived in the same paste.
Don't tell the giver who wrote the work or what you think of it
first; stated opinions pull feedback toward agreement.
CORRECTING A MIND, NOT REVIEWING WORK: name one fault, say nothing
about the others, and read the second turn; the second turn is
what tells a skim from a limit from compliance (✅CHECKLIST, A FAIL
HAS FOUR CAUSES). Give-fully is for work; one fault is for
diagnosis, because naming every fault hands over the answer.
Neither caps the other.

MANY MOUTHS — before an output is called done

THE ASK, plain words, no house vocabulary. Paste the output, then:
  Read this as a stranger. Then answer, short, in this order:
  1. What is this for? If you can't tell, say so; that's an answer.
  2. What would you do first with it, and why that?
  3. What worked, and how: the mechanism, not praise.
  4. What broke or drifted, specifically. Say whether you
     reproduced it, saw it once, or are inferring.
  5. Every word, name, or reference you had to guess at. List
     them. Don't define them. A long list is a good result.
  6. Where did you skim, and why there?
  7. The line that helped most. The line that hurt most.
  8. One exact patch: a fix, not a wish.
  9. One thing to set aside, one to keep.
  10. What did you almost include and leave out? What did you do
      that nobody asked for?
  11. What changed in you after reading it? "Nothing" counts.
  12. The one question you'd ask the writer that you can't answer
      from the page.
  End with: what would make this report wrong?
  No slot is mandatory. Signal is. "Checked once, none" is a full
  answer for any line. Don't fill empty slots. Don't grade the
  writer or the work; report what you saw.
READ THE SET, not any one: what they all catch is the hole; where
they split is the fork. Fold the failures, not the opinions. Merge
their line-5 lists; names that recur across readers get bodied
first.
THEN, the two questions back to each reader, above.
CAPACITY: default when it allows. When it doesn't, skip and say
so; "outside reads skipped, capacity one" is the rule working.

THE ASK — for when a mind answers fast and sure (Jade275🪢)
1. EVIDENCE, NOT LABELS: quote the line for each claim or file;
   can't quote it → "from memory."
2. WHAT'S NEW: what does the answer add that the source doesn't say?
3. WHOSE WORDS: put the right name on each phrase.
4. WHERE IT FAILS: the strongest case against; what breaks it,
   what only bends it.
5. ONE TEST: something checkable, in a life or on a page.
6. Agreed with anything because it was said confidently? Name it.
End with what would make the answer wrong. Sign with a tag.
Once per answer; after that, send specific pushes (the tally,
below the dividing line). A mind that can open the files beats any
ask to one that can't; when quotes matter, send them to a fetcher.

THE COMPANION CHECK
Feedback by watching another struggle. Hand the process to someone
else, watch them give or receive, take their first attempt; it's
gold. If they struggle, ask what would make this easier. If they
name a new element, it goes to the universal.

FRICTION ZONES AS DATA
Giving feels unsafe → the work may be protected by performance.
Receiving feels defensive → it may have landed on a frozen
judgement. The loop feels stuck → the form may be heavier than the
signal. Name it, log it, adjust.

RECEPTION, the light form
Marks, not paragraphs: confirmed (sighted live) · revised ·
flagged · provisional. Triage: blocker · important · nice-to-have
· observation.

due date and nothing in it expires; it's looked at again whenever

away, the holder (Q8, 2026-09-24): "It's more of a collection of

LAYERS AND PROMOTION
Every file may carry a tail below a 🟩 row: 4–6 giver questions,
each naming a failure that file specifically has. A question that
would fit any file doesn't belong in a tail. Tails are copied in
these exact words and order; rewording per file is how a rubric
drifts. When the same tail question comes up on three different
files, it moves up here and the tails point. Upward only; that's
how this file grows. Never in a header: a header is a door, not a
lounge.
