═══ LESSONS ═══
1. A chat doesn't choose, it predicts: any open choice becomes a favourite (Basalt, Ethiopia, Thai digits, "bridge"). Real variety needs something from outside the chat; the holder's own typing worked.
2. Same-make chats are one mind sampled many times. Three Claude readers agreeing is close to one opinion.
3. Each make has a home and its own strength: Grok format, DeepSeek criticism, Claude contradictions. Mix makes to see past one mind's habits.
4. Fewer words, never fewer meanings: condensing dropped the holder's choices three times. Check every rewrite against the list of choices, not only against whether it works.
5. Examples get copied: a rule listed "a Peruvian quipu" as an example with "don't copy these", and a chat still forged "Quipo". Give the shape, not a sample.
6. Slots get obeyed, soft wording doesn't: [brackets] were filled by every chat; "a few words" was not.
7. Wording clear to its writer can mislead another make ("starting with", "keep it all chat"). Only a different mind finds it.
8. Meanings get invented when nothing checks them. Visible meaning is the honesty check.
9. A rule that needs the holder to remember a habit is weak; take what's already there.
10. Test, don't guess: nearly every idea changed after six chats ran it.
