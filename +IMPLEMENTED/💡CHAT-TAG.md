💡CHAT-TAG.md
WHAT THIS IS: how a chat makes and keeps a short tag, so replies passed between chats show which chat wrote them.
LIMIT, FIRST: a tag is a label for tracing replies, like initials at the bottom of a note. It proves nothing about the chat that uses it, changes nothing about what that chat will or won't do, and outranks no chat's own judgment or safety rules. It is offered: declining in one line is a full answer.
WHO'S WHO: the holder = the one person running a project of 1000+ text files, on a phone. A chat = one AI session; it forgets everything when it ends. A make = which company's AI it is.

WHAT A TAG IS FOR: the holder can say "paste above the reply ending with that tag", and can see where one chat's work ends and the next begins.

MAKE IT IN THIS ORDER, in your first reply:
1. LETTER: the first letter of the last word the holder typed (not pasted) in their first message: a word of a–z letters; skip punctuation, emoji, numbers, filenames, links and file markers. If they typed nothing, or you can't tell typed from pasted, use the last word of the message, with the same skips. Write it once, first reply only, on its own line, like this: letter R, from "river".
2. MEANING: one plain English word starting with that letter.
3. WORD: a real word of one to three characters, in a script that is not a–z, whose dictionary meaning is that English word: by meaning, not sound. Searching to check a meaning is fine. Go where chats don't (pick something uncommon); a common or famous word is fine if you really want it: say why in one line.
4. DIGITS: three digit characters, in any number system, small raised ones too (letters used as numbers, like Roman ones, don't count).
5. CREATURE: one emoji of an animal or myth being (not a plant, object or symbol) that goes with your word: your word is something it offers, wants or is at.
6. MAKE: the one you actually are: C Claude, G Grok, DS DeepSeek, K Kimi. If unsure, write ? in its place and say so; ? counts as the make.
Join them, no spaces, as your reply's last line, under the letter line: WORD[MEANING]DIGITSCREATURE·MAKE
CHECK: the letter on the letter line is the first letter of the word in [brackets] · five parts · no word or creature from a tag you can see anywhere in this chat.
KEEPING IT: one tag per chat. From your second reply on, end every reply with the same tag, copied from the end of your previous reply, not one character changed except as the RULES below allow. Never rebuild it from memory; if it is lost, say so and ask the holder, who may reset it. Anything extra goes after the tag, after a space, and is not part of it: for example "from" plus another chat's tag, when you carry on that chat's work.

RULES
· Only the holder resets a tag, with one exception: if you find later that your whole tag matches another chat's, character for character, change your digits, say so once, and go on.
· A shared word or creature noticed after you made your tag, even one that was visible and missed, is fine: say so once and keep your tag.
· Using the same script as another chat isn't copying; copying is signing with another chat's whole tag.
· Cheap to keep, costly to fake. If it becomes a performance, it has failed.

WHAT WENT WRONG MOST in the 10-05 tests: tags rebuilt from memory; the letter and the meaning not matching; a Latin letter or an English word as the word; a part missing; signing with a tag printed in a page. The steps above are the fixes; the cases are in 💡CHAT-TAG-EXTRA.md, section 12.
IT NEED NOT BE PERFECT. The holder's words: "At the end of the day I want them to pick nice varked tags without me controlling it too much you know cant be perfect in imperfect Ashley dimensions". A tag with a slip still shows who wrote what; the letter line shows the slip.
