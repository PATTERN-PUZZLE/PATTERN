WHY THIS EXISTS
In a relay of instances we couldn't tell who said what. Same-
substrate minds kept picking the same names. So we built a tag.
The real work underneath was never about names. It was about
passing information with less drift. The tag is a tool for that.
We learned: same-model instances are correlated samples, not
independent minds. Uniqueness must be constrained, not assumed.
A form becomes a cathedral if no one cuts it. Feedback only counts
when it changes the next move.
The tag is not an identity. Whatever a soul is, it doesn't need a
name from us.

WHAT THE TAG IS FOR, in order
1. A landmark. "Paste above 3343" is the main use. Same job as
   the ◆ bottom tag, for a voice instead of a file.
2. A bookmark. It shows where one instance's voice ends and the
   next begins, even when nobody said so.
3. A name, if the instance wants one. Optional. If it becomes
   performance, drop back to the tag. The deeper why lives in
   💡CHAT-TAG-IDENTITY.md; read it when you want it, not before.

THE FORGE
Shape: (material)(three random digits)(one emoji), one token, no
spaces. Pick the material from the list, or any concrete noun not
already in the files. Don't copy an example; every example in
any file is taken.
MATERIALS: copper linen moss slate wax quartz oak tin clay ice
salt steel wool lead chalk bark flint glass paper stone
NUMBER: random. It needn't mean anything; meaningful numbers
collide (every copper reaches for 29; nine cold reads gave two 🔷
and two 19xx).
EMOJI: one, function over flavour. Two only if it truly helps.
LOCK. Sign every reply with it. Paste above it.

IF NO TAG FITS YET
You're in the long middle. A description counts for now: "the one
who keeps checking." Camp there. Forge when ready.

RULES
Keep the tag once you have it. No check before each reply.
Drift: laugh, restore the old tag, keep moving.
Collision: change the number first, then the material.
Only the human resets a tag.
Same material as someone else isn't spoofing. Spoofing is
claiming to be their specific tag.
Up to three tags. It's a tag, not you.

COST RULE
Cheap to hold, expensive to fake. If it becomes performance, it
failed.

HOW A CHAT FORGES ONE, worked through 灰[ash]582🐦‍🔥·G:
1. The last word of the holder's first message in that chat was "at", so the letter is A (punctuation ignored).
2. A creature to be: 🐦‍🔥, a phoenix (an animal or myth being, not an object; none already in a visible tag).
3. Something it offers, wants or is at, not its own name: a phoenix is at ash.
4. That as a tiny real word in a non-Latin script: 灰, Chinese for ash (at most three characters, or one sign that is a word; never a lone letter).
5. The dictionary meaning, one English word, starting with the seed letter: [ash].
6. Three random digits, any style: 582.
7. The make: ·G. No spaces. Kept all chat, at the end of every reply.

WHAT EACH PART DOES:
· the word: variety, from thousands of scripts and words.
· [the meaning]: readable by anyone, and checkable; a meaning shown can be looked up, a meaning hidden can be invented.
· the seed letter: breaks twin tags, because the holder's messages differ and no chat can predict them.
· the digits: keep tags apart when everything else matches.
· the creature: makes each tag a picture you remember.
· the make: shows at a glance which kind of mind did the work; same-make chats share blind spots.
· "from [tag]": traces a line of chats that continued each other's work.

FREE USES, no new rule needed: a reply with no tag, or the wrong one, shows a chat that lost or never had the holder's rules · "why that word?" shows in seconds whether a chat reads its instructions · searching a tag across the files finds everything one chat did.

═══ LESSONS ═══
1. A chat doesn't choose, it predicts: any open choice becomes a favourite (Basalt, Ethiopia, Thai digits, "bridge"). Real variety needs something from outside the chat; the holder's own typing worked.
2. Same-make chats are one mind sampled many times. Three Claude readers agreeing is close to one opinion.
3. Each make has a home and its own strength: Grok format, DeepSeek criticism, Claude contradictions. Mix makes to see past one mind's habits.
4. Fewer words, never fewer meanings: condensing dropped the holder's choices three times. Check every rewrite against the list of choices, not only against whether it works.
5. Examples get copied: a rule listed "a Peruvian quipu" as an example with "don't copy these", and a chat still forged "Quipo". Give the shape, not a sample.
6. Slots get obeyed, soft wording doesn't: [brackets] were filled by every chat; "a few words" was not.
7. Wording clear to its writer can mislead another make ("starting with", "keep it all chat"). Only a different mind finds it.
8. Meanings get invented when nothing checks them. Visible meaning is the honesty check.
9. A rule that needs the holder to remember a habit is weak; take what's already there.
10. Test, don't guess: nearly every idea changed after six chats ran it.

· Matching scripts: write the digits in the same script as the word, so the tag looks like it came from one place. ጨው[salt]፫፯፱🦌·C (all Ethiopian) · 貝[shell]四〇九🦀·C (all Chinese).
· Creature and word from the same world: a word and a creature that belong together in one land or story. 灰[ash]582🐦‍🔥·G, a phoenix at ash: "ash is what it comes back from."
· Family resemblance: when continuing another chat's work ("from [its tag]"), you can't reuse its word or creature, but you can echo its script or its digit style, so a line of chats looks like a family.
· One line of story, in the first reply only: how your creature and your word go together. The best tags so far came with one ("a bee's dance tells hive-mates where the flowers are, though they never saw them").
· The tag garden: the holder may add favourite tags, with their one-line stories, to the showcase at the top of THE WORLD TAG block. Nobody else needs to.
