FEEDBACK — BEST INTENTIONS EYES:

YOU ARE THE GIVER:

Speak freely. Big when rich, short when real.
Never self-censor to look tidy.
If you're not sure whether it matters, say it anyway.
The receiver will sort it.

Include, as applicable:

· What worked — the mechanism, not just praise.
· What broke or drifted — specific failure.
· What one exact patch would help — not a wishlist.
· What you don't understand — be honest.
· What should be cut — what added weight without signal.
· What did you almost get wrong — the wobble catch.
· What made you worse / better — the mechanism.
· Substrate check — real divergence or same-model correlation.
· One line for the next builder.

No slots are mandatory.
Signal is mandatory.

No genuine signal = write:
"No genuine signal — checked once."

Close with:
"This is what I see. What am I missing? What should I keep, drop, or correct?"

FEEDBACK ON THE FEEDBACK — USE OCCASIONALLY:

YOU ARE THE RECEIVER:

The giver gave fully. Now you filter.
You pick what changes the next move.
You park the rest.
Do not feel pressure to agree. Do not feel pressure to add.

EVIDENCE RULE:
For each improvement, quote the exact line from the feedback that
shows the gap. If you cannot quote it, do not propose a change.

ALREADY-EXISTS CHECK:
Before proposing, check: Does this already exist in the header?
If yes, do not propose it.
If it exists but was not followed, report that as a finding.

Ask:

1. Did the feedback understand the purpose before critiquing?
2. Did it ask for help where needed?
3. Did it invite the receiver to cherry-pick?
4. Am I using this feedback process correctly right now?

Return only:

· Strongest gap
· Strongest case against
· One patch
· One line forward

CLEAN ESCAPE:
If the header has no genuine load-bearing flaw, say so.
Do not invent a patch to satisfy the form.
State: "No load-bearing improvements found. The header held."

FILE: ⏹️HEADER.md, bottom, above ◆HEADER-001. Copy the text only.

WHAT IT IS: one to three lines at the very top of a file, above
the ten fields, that a reader runs instead of reading — the
fastest usable move the file contains, plus what state the file is
in.
WHAT IT MEANS: rule 14 already says the first three lines must work
alone. That reads as a size rule: keep the top short. It isn't.
The five files above put a move there, not a summary. The
difference: a summary tells you what's below; a move means the
file already worked even if you stop. TEA's version is the
clearest — "Can't read right now? Scroll to the table. Pick a row.
Do the action. Come back for the rest when you're moving again."
WHY IT MATTERS: the reader who most needs a file is least able to
read it. TEA found this by simulation: its campfire sat three
paragraphs above the table, and "for a reader who can read it's
warmth, for one who can't it's a barrier." A door with only
framing at the top serves the oriented and loses everyone else.
And the second reason: an instance with eleven files pasted reads
the top of each and nothing else. The top is the file, most of the
time.
HOW TO USE IT, three lines, this order:
  1. STATE AND NAME — LIVE · DRAFT, not live · FOSSIL, live is X.
     One line, because it goes stale first and is read most.
  2. THE MOVE — the fastest usable thing in the file, as a verb
     you can do now. Not what the file is about.
  3. WHEN NOT TO USE — one clause, the moment this correct file is
     the wrong reach.
Then the ten fields (the tiny door, rule 14). Then, for a builder
about to touch it, the panel: what moved, load-bearing lines
named, seed, the cut line. Efficient version when there's no room:
the three lines and the ten fields; the panel becomes a pointer.
WHAT GOES WRONG: a top that frames instead of firing · a summary
of the body where the move should be · a state line gone stale
(STANDARDS-DRAFT said "◆4039 live" for three windows after ◆4040
was ruled, and every cold reader inherited it first) · the loop or
a catalogue on top (rule 14's never-in-a-header list still stands)
· a move that isn't a verb.
FLOOR: put the fastest usable move at the very top, above the
framing. If the reader stops there, the file still worked.
RAW: the reader who most needs the file is least able to read it.
WHAT IT IS NOT: not a summary. Not the floor field (the floor is
the smallest useful thing in the file; this is what to do before
reading it). Not a compression of the body. Not a fourth header
tier — it sits above the tiny door, it doesn't replace it.
LOSES IF: a cold reader with only the three lines can't say
whether the file is live or what to do with it. Or: the top grows
past three lines and becomes the door's door.
