- What does the affected person say?
- What was the first snag?
- Did the correction become pressure, shame, hierarchy, or false certainty?

“Nothing changed,” “this did not help,” and “this caused a new problem” are valuable findings.

### 7. HAND OFF — Preserve a reusable case

The unit other minds inherit should be:

> **Scenario + source scope + stakes + independent readings + action + observed result + first snag + correction + unresolved edges.**

That is a practice case. A conclusion without those elements is only another candidate description.

## The higher-stakes boundary

For health, law, safety, finance, coercion, crisis, or decisions affecting vulnerable people:

**The patterns may generate questions; they do not make the decision.**

Use relevant domain evidence, applicable rules, qualified human judgment, appropriate local support, and independent scrutiny proportionate to the possible harm. Serious or irreversible decisions need documented evidence and accountable review. Immediate danger requires practical safety or emergency help before metaphysical or pattern analysis.

A useful rule is:

> **Do not “graduate” into higher stakes because the vocabulary has been mastered. Increase support only after demonstrated transfer, successful small-scale use, and the addition of domain competence.**

## A ready-to-use rehearsal prompt

Use this in separate fresh chats. Do not show the helpers one another’s answers first. This is a synthetic rehearsal, so nobody should claim an observed real-world result.

```text
INDEPENDENT GOLD-OS PRACTICE REHEARSAL

Answer independently. Do not assume other helpers agree with you.
This is a synthetic rehearsal, not a live intervention or evidence that
the method works.

SCENARIO

A six-person community project relies on long shared notes. New volunteers
are missing handoffs.

The coordinator proposes three changes:

1. Every update must fit into three lines.
2. Uncertainties and unresolved disagreements must be removed from the
   main note so newcomers are not confused.
3. Anyone who misses a handoff must explain the failure publicly.

One volunteer says the uncertainty notes have prevented repeated mistakes.
Another says public explanations would make them anxious and less willing
to ask for help.

The coordinator wants reliable handoffs without hiding important conditions
or pressuring people. A two-week reversible trial is possible.

DESIRED BETTER OUTCOME

Reliable, findable handoffs that preserve necessary uncertainty and allow
people to ask for help without shame.

TASK

Use this basic path:

SOURCE → STAKES → SPLIT → MOVE → CHECK → HAND OFF

Return these fields:

1. SOURCE HELD:
   Facts directly present in the scenario.

2. SOURCE MISSING:
   The most important information you do not hold.

3. PEOPLE AND STAKES:
   Who could benefit, who could pay a cost, and whose refusal or experience
   needs direct attention.

4. FIRST MOVE:
   Your initial proposed action before comparing with other helpers.

5. PATTERN OR DISTINCTION:
   Name no more than two useful patterns or distinctions, if any.
   Do not force the scenario to illustrate a whole framework.

6. INVERSE / DISCONFIRMING READING:
   A plausible way your reading or proposed correction could be wrong.

7. SMALLEST REVERSIBLE MOVE:
   One concrete two-week trial, including who must be consulted.

8. STOP CONDITION:
   What observable result would require stopping or changing the trial.

9. WHAT TO OBSERVE:
   Specific qualitative evidence of help, no help, or harm.
   Do not use fluency, agreement, or vocabulary repetition as evidence.

10. HANDOFF:
    What a later reader would need to inspect and correct your proposal.

Do not claim that the trial occurred, that people consented, or that the
outcome improved. Mark those as future observations.
```

```text
Compare these independent rehearsal logs without averaging them into
consensus or treating agreement as proof.

Identify:
- different first moves;
- missing source questions;
- anyone whose cost or refusal was overlooked;
- the strongest reversible elements;
- unsupported assumptions;

- possible stop conditions;

Do not claim an observed outcome. Preserve substantive disagreement.
```
